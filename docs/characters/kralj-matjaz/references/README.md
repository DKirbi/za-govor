# Kralj Matjaž — research references

## Scholarly sources

1. **Monika Kropej Telban**, “Razvojna pot in percepcija izročil o Kralju Matjažu in Petru Klepcu,” in *Močni, modri in dobri: Junaki v slovenski folklori*, ed. Božidar Jezernik and Ingrid Slavec Gradišnik, Znanstvena založba Filozofske fakultete, Ljubljana, 2019, pp. 35–59. Open access: https://www.dlib.si/stream/URN%3ANBN%3ASI%3ADOC-XS68CL98/bf50b163-d5e4-4c07-aafc-34648d0f8435/PDF  
   Useful for the layered development of the tradition, sleeping-hero and mountain motifs, the wider European context, and the association with Matthias Corvinus.

2. **Marija Klobčar**, “Prezrti zgodovinski kontekst izročila o kralju Matjažu,” *Studia mythologica Slavica* (2023). https://doi.org/10.3986/SMS20232604  
   Reconsiders the historical context and proposes looking for an earlier, pre-Corvinus historical layer. Treat this as a scholarly argument, not consensus.

3. **Ivan Grafenauer**, *Slovenske pripovedke o Kralju Matjažu*, SAZU, Dela 4, Ljubljana, 1951. Foundational collection and study of Slovenian narratives.

## Museum and heritage sources

4. **Slovenski etnografski muzej**, digital collection entry for Leopold Layer's beehive panel depicting Kralj Matjaž: https://www.etno-muzej.si/sl/digitalne-zbirke/panjske-koncnice/630lju0002338e  
   Identifies the sleeping king, cave, beard winding around the table, and the painting's Layer workshop tradition. The museum notes uncertainty around the exact date inscription.

5. **I feel Slovenia**, “Slovenski miti in legende” (Kralj Matjaž): https://www.slovenia.info/sl/zgodbe/raziscite-slovenski-svet-mitov-in-legend  
   Current public retelling of the Peca cave, nine turns of the beard, and the winter linden motif. Useful as a record of the contemporary popular version, not as a substitute for folklore scholarship.

6. **Hiša Matevž**, “Kralj Matjaž”: https://www.hisamatevz.si/kralj-matjaz/  
   Local tourism presentation of the Peca tradition and the route to the cave. Use for local context only.

## Research cautions

- The tradition is widespread across Central and Eastern Europe and has multiple variants.
- The name is commonly associated with Matthias Corvinus, but scholarship debates the older layers and historical interpretation.
- The beard's number of turns is not uniform in every telling. Nine is prominent in the contemporary Peca version; the Slovenski etnografski muzej's panel description records the motif without fixing the count.
- The winter linden is not present in every variant.
- Do not present the cave statue, modern tourist installations, or the annual snow-castle festival as folkloric evidence for the original narrative.
