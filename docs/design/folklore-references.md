# Folklore and Mythology References

**Purpose:** Establish a research standard and a traceable register for folkloric, mythological, literary and historical content in *Za govor!*.

**Important:** This is a working register, not a claim that every character below has already been fully researched. A subject must not be treated as visually or narratively verified until its individual source record is completed.

## 1. Research principles

For every character, creature, item or story:
1. Identify the exact name, spelling, variants and subject type.
2. Identify the region, community, period and source tradition.
3. Find primary or scholarly sources where possible.
4. Record what is directly documented, what is a later retelling, and what remains uncertain.
5. Gather visual references only when their provenance is known.
6. Separate folklore canon, game interpretation and gameplay fiction.
7. Cite sources in the concept/specification.

Preferred source hierarchy:
- Scholarly folklore and mythology research; critical editions and academic publications.
- Published folklore collections with named collectors, informants, dates and locations.
- Museums, archives, libraries, universities and cultural heritage institutions.
- Reputable local cultural institutions and documented heritage projects.
- Secondary popular sources only as leads, not sole evidence for contested claims.

A search result, unsourced illustration, fantasy wiki or AI-generated description is not sufficient evidence.

## 2. Three layers of truth

### Folklore canon
What a traceable source documents: story, motif, name, attributes, regional context, ritual or representation.

### Game interpretation
A deliberate artistic adaptation: silhouette, palette, pose, simplified costume, visual metaphor or staging. Explain the source basis and the adaptation.

### Gameplay fiction
Original game mechanics and events: party combinations, battles, skill effects, encounters and invented dialogue. These may be inspired by source material but must not be described as historical or folkloric fact.

Characters from separate stories or periods may adventure together. This is game fiction, not evidence that they met.

## 5. Items, symbols and motifs

An item name referencing a deity or tradition does not prove that the item historically belonged to that figure. For example, the progression document distinguishes Perun's axe from Svarog's smithing hammer; retain that distinction as the current design rationale, but verify it against appropriate scholarship before presenting it as a broad historical fact.

For every relic, costume element, weapon, symbol or motif:
- Verify its source and regional use.
- Distinguish documented object from artistic shorthand.
- Do not invent historical equipment or iconography.
- Label original game designs as interpretations.

## 6. Research tasks before approval

- Build a full bibliography for each playable hero and god.
- Create separate source records for each enemy/creature.
- Verify the source basis for Lintvern and the Ljubljana dragon finale.
- Verify each story illustration against a specific story version.
- Review all item names and symbolic associations.
- Record regional variants rather than flattening them into one universal version.

## 7. Approval rule

No substantial new folkloric character, creature, story illustration, equipment or ability is final until the evidence, uncertainty and game interpretation have been presented to the Game Director. A compelling design is not a substitute for a reliable source.
