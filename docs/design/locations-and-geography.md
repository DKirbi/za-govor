# Locations and Geography — Za govor!

**Purpose:** Keep the game's stylized environments faithful to real Slovenian geography, architecture and cultural context.

**Status:** The route below is the current project route. The landmark descriptions are drawn from existing project documentation and are starting points for visual research, not completed geographic verification.

## 1. Non-negotiable principle

Stylization may change lighting, season, weather, atmosphere and composition. It must not make the real place geographically implausible.

Never combine unrelated landmarks, invent a viewpoint, move a mountain behind a town for convenience, or imply that distant places are adjacent when they are not.

## 3. Location research checklist

For every background or substantial environment revision:
1. Define the exact real-world location and intended camera/viewpoint.
2. Find reliable photographs from that viewpoint or a clearly identified nearby viewpoint.
3. Consult maps and terrain data to understand elevation, orientation and spatial relationships.
4. Verify architecture, vegetation, geological features and landmarks.
5. Check whether all visible landmarks could plausibly appear together.
6. Record the source links and any unavoidable artistic simplification.
7. Present the concept for Game Director approval.

Preferred sources:
- Surveying and mapping authorities, including GURS and official map/terrain services.
- National, regional and local museums and heritage institutions.
- Official park, municipality and tourism heritage sources for local context.
- Landscape, geology and ecology publications where relevant.
- Dated, geographically identifiable photographs and archival images.

Tourism images can help with visual orientation but should not be the sole evidence for historical or ecological claims.

## 5. Known pitfalls

Two previously identified examples illustrate the standard:
- A Ptuj composition used Pohorje as an inappropriate backdrop. Do not repeat this without verifying the actual view.
- A Bled composition showed Triglav from a geographically implausible viewpoint. A recognizable landmark should not be inserted simply because it is iconic.

These are design cautions, not a substitute for researching each new composition.

## 6. Real geography vs game fiction

The road is a designed game journey. Its order, transitions, encounters and pacing are gameplay choices. Do not imply that the player is following a historically documented route or that geographically separated folkloric events occurred together.

The Ljubljana Castle battle against Lintvern is the game's fictional finale. Any claimed local legend connecting a dragon to a specific place must be separately sourced.

## 7. Approval

A background is not geographically approved merely because it looks convincing. Present the reference view, source links, intended stylization and any uncertainties to the Game Director before treating a substantial environment concept as final.
