# Art Direction — Za govor!

**Status:** CONFIRMED overall visual direction. Individual concepts and assets still require review and approval.

## 1. Visual identity

The approved direction is **Hybrid**: modernized retro pixel art for the playable world, combined with traditional folk-art illustration for selected storytelling and collectible elements.

The game should feel like a cohesive Slovene folklore adventure, not a generic fantasy game with folkloric decoration.

## 2. Pixel-art language

Use modernized SNES/GBA-inspired pixel art for:
- Gameplay characters and enemies.
- Journey environments and landmarks.
- Road objects, props, items and icons.
- Battle actions, status effects and visual feedback.

Priorities:
- Clear, recognizable silhouettes at small sizes.
- Consistent pixel density, scale and outline treatment.
- Deliberate palettes and readable value contrast.
- Expressive poses and animation without visual noise.
- Effects that communicate gameplay state and impact.
- Assets that remain legible on mobile screens.

Avoid mixing incompatible pixel scales, excessive gradients that undermine the pixel language, noisy texture and tiny decorative details that disappear at gameplay size.

## 3. Folk-art illustration

Use traditional folk-art illustration for:
- Folklore story panels and collectible artwork.
- Selected narrative moments and character/story cards.
- Other approved elements where a hand-painted tradition adds meaning.

Panjske končnice (painted beehive-front panels) are an important visual reference. They are a reference for composition, stylization and storytelling—not a requirement to imitate a specific historical panel or to apply the style to every screen.

Illustrations should be researched and should communicate a particular story or documented motif. Do not invent a traditional-looking image and label it authentic.

## 4. UI direction

The UI is restrained, functional and readable:
- Dark surfaces.
- Gold accents.
- Subtle borders and ornament where they clarify hierarchy or reinforce identity.
- Clear typography and strong contrast.
- Consistent spacing and predictable interaction patterns.

Do not decorate every panel. Avoid ornamental frames, particles, symbols, dividers or animations that compete with the word puzzle or add no functional/narrative value.

Whitespace is intentional. Visual polish does not mean filling every empty area.

## 5. Battle composition and UX

Battle scenes should feel expressive and atmospheric, like small RPG story moments, while preserving the puzzle as the primary interaction.

- Keep the letter grid, word construction and controls unobstructed.
- Make hero/enemy silhouettes and battle states easy to read.
- Give attacks satisfying visual feedback, scaled to their gameplay importance.
- Effects must not obscure letters or essential information.
- Keep mobile touch targets and text comfortably readable.
- Use hierarchy and negative space rather than adding more decoration.

The scene should support the word mechanic, not compete with it.

## 6. Environment art

Stylization may change lighting, season, weather, time of day and atmosphere. It must not falsify the real geography.

Before designing a location:
- Identify the exact place and intended viewpoint.
- Verify terrain, skyline, architecture, vegetation and nearby landmarks.
- Check whether the depicted landmarks could plausibly be visible together.
- Use reference photographs, maps and institutional/local sources.
- Preserve the recognizable identity of the place.

Do not add famous mountains, castles or churches as generic decoration if they do not belong in the chosen view.

## 7. Character and creature art

Every folkloric or historical subject requires research before substantial concept work. Verify regional origin, source tradition, documented attributes and available visual references.

Do not invent weapons, clothing, equipment, symbols, powers or attacks and present them as authentic. Game-specific visual interpretation is allowed when clearly separated from source material and approved by the Game Director.

## 8. Asset review checklist

Before approval, check:
- Does it fit the Hybrid direction?
- Is the silhouette readable at actual gameplay scale?
- Is pixel density consistent with neighbouring assets?
- Is the palette coherent and contrast sufficient?
- Does it communicate its gameplay or narrative purpose?
- Does it preserve the real subject's recognizable features?
- Are cultural/geographic claims supported?
- Does it remain legible on mobile?
- Does any decoration distract from the core interaction?

## 9. Status discipline

The Hybrid direction is **CONFIRMED**. This does not mean every existing prototype asset is approved. The progression document describes some current art as prototype art requiring further direction. Review existing assets before replacing or redesigning them.
